# Archive Methodology

## Why this archive exists

**[LESSON]** A repository shows *what* was built. It does not show *why*, what was abandoned, or what was learned. This archive preserves the reasoning.

## Principles used

1. **Write it from the beginning** — including the parts that did not work.
2. **Preserve decisions with their reasoning**, not only their outcomes.
3. **Do not polish.** The record is: experiments → mistakes → corrections → learning → decisions → transition.
4. **Distinguish the categories:**
   - **[FACT]** — something actually built, used, tested, or decided
   - **[EXPERIMENT]** — something explored but not necessarily completed
   - **[LESSON]** — something learned from the experience
   - **[FUTURE IDEA]** — something considered but not implemented
5. **Do not invent dates.** Use *early phase*, *later phase*, *during the project*, *as the platform evolved*, *eventually*.
6. **Do not manufacture metrics.** No user counts, revenue, or achievements are invented.
7. **Preserve uncertainty.** Where information is missing, the text says: **Details not available in the current archive context.**
8. **Separate assets from infrastructure.** Code and knowledge are kept; rented infrastructure is retired.

## Tone

Honest, technical, reflective, professional. Not self-congratulatory. Not apologetic. TechStudyHubCore is not described as a successful startup, and not described as a failure. It was an independent technical and product learning ecosystem and experiment.

## Intended reader

The author's future self — and, if one forms, a future team, who should be able to read this and understand:

> What did Kartik actually build, why did he build it, what happened, and why did he eventually stop?

rather than simply seeing an old repository.

## How to maintain it

- Update `09_SHUTDOWN/Final-State.md` at the moment of shutdown.
- Add to `07_DECISIONS/` whenever a significant decision is made.
- Keep `10_FUTURE/Conditions-for-Revival.md` as the gate for any revival.
