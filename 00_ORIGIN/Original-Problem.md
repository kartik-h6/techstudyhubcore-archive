# The Original Problem

## Statement

**[FACT]** The problem the project set out to address:

> Learning material exists, but it is often unclear, unstructured, not searchable, and not built for understanding. Notes are collected, not organised for comprehension.

The practical question that followed:

> How can learning material become clearer, more structured, visual, searchable and useful to students — rather than simply becoming another collection of notes?

## Honest caveat — the problem was assumed, not validated

**[LESSON]** The problem was identified primarily from personal experience as a student. It was a reasonable assumption, but it was not validated with real users before significant building began.

That ordering mistake is one of the most important lessons preserved in this archive — see `06_PRODUCT/Validation-Lessons.md`.

## Why the problem was chosen

- It was a problem the author personally experienced, which made starting easy
- It required content and effort rather than capital
- It could be built with accessible static web technology
- It served audiences the author understood

## Why it turned out to be harder than expected

- Content volume grows faster than the capacity to produce it well
- Users and search engines reward depth and coherence, not page count
- A free educational audience does not convert to support easily
- A broad audience (pharmacy, nursing, school science) implies multiple content standards, not one
